About

A statistician's foundation, a data scientist's toolkit.

I'm a statistician and data scientist with training in economics, statistics, and data science & analytics. My academic and research experiences have shaped my interest in using statistical and computational methods to understand complex, real-world problems.

My work spans statistical inference, causal analysis, applied statistical modeling, machine learning, and data visualization. Across projects involving public health, policy, behavioral outcomes, socioeconomic conditions, and education, I've worked with large and complex datasets to identify patterns, evaluate relationships, and communicate evidence clearly.

I bring together a quantitative foundation with practical computational skills in Python, R, SQL, and Stata to approach research questions from both methodological and applied angles — whether that means a regression table, a reproducible analysis pipeline, or a dashboard someone outside the field can actually use.

Research Focus

What I study, and how I study it.

My research interests lie at the intersection of statistics and computation. I'm particularly interested in statistical modeling, statistical inference, survey methodology, and computational statistics, with a broader interest in how these methods can be used to understand complex, real-world data.

I'm interested in applying rigorous quantitative methods to questions across public health, policy, education, behavioral research, and other data-intensive domains. Through continued research and doctoral training, I hope to deepen my methodological expertise while contributing to research that produces meaningful, interpretable, and evidence-based insights.

Academic Plagiarism & Retraction Analysis

2026

Poverty & Firearm Violence, State-Level

Integrated 286,420 incident-level records with Census poverty and income data across all 50 states. A permutation test (10,000 iterations) found that high-poverty states averaged 26.28 incidents per 100,000 residents, compared with 13.60 in low-poverty states — a statistically significant difference.

Data: Gun Violence Archive + U.S. Census ACS  ·  Methods: nonparametric permutation testing

RPermutation TestingCensus Data

2026

Adolescent Risk Behavior, 1991–2017

Applied Random Forest, XGBoost, and CatBoost with SHAP interpretability to three decades of CDC Youth Risk Behavior data (1.17M+ records). The models identified notable sex-based differences in risk-behavior prevalence, with alcohol use emerging as the most consistent risk category across survey years.

Data: CDC Youth Risk Behavior Surveillance System  ·  Methods: ensemble ML, SHAP feature importance

XGBoostRandom ForestSHAP
